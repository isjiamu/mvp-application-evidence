# ModelScope Cookbook · 魔搭紫皮书

## Activity narrative

Which open-source model should we choose—and how do we actually use it inside a company?

We kept hearing these questions from enterprise teams. After about a month of collaborative work, we released the [ModelScope Cookbook](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-cookbook) (《魔搭紫皮书》), an open-source practical guide created together with the ModelScope community.

Following our WorkBuddy Guide and Doubao Work Guide, this project takes our shared learning resources deeper into the model layer: selecting, running, adapting and evaluating models for real applications.

The starting point was a familiar gap. People know many model names, but getting their first model running raises a different set of questions. Where do you download it? What hardware does it need? How do you prepare business data? How do you decide whether the result is useful?

We organized the guide around that journey:

- Translate a business need into a model task.
- Choose a model and establish an evaluation baseline.
- Run inference locally or in a cloud notebook.
- Prepare data, fine-tune and compare the results.
- Connect models to practical applications, including knowledge Q&A, speech assistants, customer-service quality analysis and generative AI workflows.

I helped co-create the cookbook and also contributed to its reading experience, improving [navigation, search and the homepage](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-cookbook/commit/1786176cc7234b1c279ee9c09d7130e20042189b). For a learning resource, helping someone find the next relevant step is part of making the knowledge usable.

One point I especially appreciate in our material is that an experiment does not have to produce a perfect result to be worth documenting. A training run may improve output formatting without improving answer accuracy. Keeping that result helps readers understand what changed and what still needs work.

Our aim is to help developers move from collecting model links to building applications they can reproduce, evaluate and improve.

Thank you to Liu Cong, the ModelScope team and all our coauthors and contributors. If you have a useful case, a correction or a reproduction note, we welcome your contribution.
